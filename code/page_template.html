Picture the world in 1900. Steam ships and railways already tie the continents together, and the prairies of North America, the Pampas of Argentina and the steppe of southern Russia are being ploughed for wheat. But most of the Brazilian Cerrado is open savanna, the forests of Sumatra and Borneo are largely intact, much of inland Australia is not yet grazed, and Europe is farmed harder than it is today. What did the land look like then, and how different is it now?

The best answer comes from HYDE, the History Database of the Global Environment, built at Utrecht University and the Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency. Its latest version, 3.3, estimates cropland, pasture and rangeland on a grid of 5 arc-minutes (about 9 km at the equator) for every decade back to 10,000 BCE and every year since 2000. For 1900 it rests on old censuses and agricultural statistics, spread over the land by population and suitability; for 2023 on FAO statistics. I set its maps for 1900 and 2023 side by side, add its anthromes (a classification of land into settled, farmed, seminatural and wild), and check the present against a satellite land cover map from MODIS.

    02 · FarmlandA fifth of the land in 1900, more than a third now

    HYDE puts the world's cropland at {{hyde.crop_1900_km2|m1}} million km² in 1900 and {{hyde.crop_2023_km2|m1}} million km² in 2023, {{hyde.crop_x|f1}} times as much. Grazing land grew faster, from {{hyde.graz_1900_km2|m1}} to {{hyde.graz_2023_km2|m1}} million km². Together that is {{hyde.farm_1900_km2|m1}} million km² of farmland in 1900 and {{hyde.farm_2023_km2|m1}} million now, out of {{hyde.icefree_km2|m0}} million km² of land free of ice (Antarctica is not on the grid). In 123 years, farming took in another {{hyde.farm_gain_km2|m1}} million km², an area {{hyde.farm_gain_vs_us|f1}} times the size of the United States.

    The ice-free land in 1900 and 2023

    Share of {{hyde.icefree_km2|m0}} million km² of ice-free land (Antarctica excluded). HYDE 3.3. Hover for values.

    Most of the new farmland is grazing land, and most of that is rangeland: natural grassland, shrubland and savanna grazed by livestock, as across inland Australia, the Sahel or the steppes of Central Asia and China. HYDE counts {{hyde.range_2023_km2|m1}} million km² of it now, against {{hyde.past_2023_km2|m1}} million km² of more intensively managed pasture and {{hyde.conv_2023_km2|m1}} million km² of converted rangeland, grazing land that was once forest. A map of grazing land is therefore not a map of cleared land: much of it would still look wild from the air.

    World cropland and grazing land, 1700 to 2016

    Million km². The long series is HYDE 3.2 (the previous version, the latest with yearly world totals I could reuse); the dots are HYDE 3.3 for 1900 and 2023. Hover for values.

    The 20th century did most of the work. In HYDE 3.2's world totals, farmland grew by {{series.farm_1700_1900_gain_km2|m1}} million km² in the two centuries from 1700 to 1900, and by {{series.farm_1900_1950_gain_km2|m1}} million km² in the half century from 1900 to 1950 alone, as railways, steamships and refrigeration opened the grasslands of the Americas, Australia and Russia to the world market. Since 1990 the world total has grown far more slowly: by {{series.farm_1990_2016_gain_km2|m1}} million km² to 2016, against {{series.farm_1950_1990_gain_km2|m1}} million in the four decades before.

    03 · Where it happenedThe tropics, the steppe and the outback

    Of {{change.n_countries|int}} countries and territories with at least 1,000 km² of land, farmland grew in all but {{change.n_farm_down|int}}. By region, the largest gains were in sub-Saharan Africa and South America, then East Asia and Oceania. Only Europe has less farmland than in 1900.

    Change in farmland by region, 1900 to 2023

    Country by country, China (+{{change.top_farm_gain.0.v|m1}} million km²), Brazil (+{{change.top_farm_gain.1.v|m1}}) and Australia (+{{change.top_farm_gain.2.v|m1}}) gained the most farmland, then the United States, Argentina and Kazakhstan. The kinds of change differ. In Brazil much of it was clearing: the Cerrado, the great savanna of the interior, went from {{change.hotspots.cerrado.farm_1900_pct|pc}} farmed in 1900 to {{change.hotspots.cerrado.farm_2023_pct|pc}} now, and the southern rim of the Amazon from {{change.hotspots.amazon_arc.farm_1900_pct|pc}} to {{change.hotspots.amazon_arc.farm_2023_pct|pc}}. In Australia and China it was mostly new grazing land, much of it dry rangeland. In Southeast Asia it was cropland: rice, rubber and oil palm took the share of farmed land from {{change.hotspots.se_asia.farm_1900_pct|pc}} to {{change.hotspots.se_asia.farm_2023_pct|pc}}.

    Some famous farm belts were already there in 1900. The US Midwest and Corn Belt was {{change.hotspots.midwest.farm_1900_pct|pc}} farmland in 1900 and is {{change.hotspots.midwest.farm_2023_pct|pc}} now: the prairie had been ploughed in the half century before. The Great Plains further west went from {{change.hotspots.great_plains.farm_1900_pct|pc}} to {{change.hotspots.great_plains.farm_2023_pct|pc}}, the Kazakh steppe, ploughed in the Soviet Virgin Lands campaign of the 1950s, from {{change.hotspots.virgin_lands.farm_1900_pct|pc}} to {{change.hotspots.virgin_lands.farm_2023_pct|pc}}, and the Argentine Pampas from {{change.hotspots.pampas.farm_1900_pct|pc}} to {{change.hotspots.pampas.farm_2023_pct|pc}}.

    04 · ForestCleared in the tropics, back in Europe and the eastern US

    HYDE does not map forest itself, but it does map where forest would grow naturally, using the WWF map of the world's biomes, and which of that forest zone is farmed. The forest zone covers {{forest.zone_km2|m1}} million km², {{forest.zone_pct_icefree|pc}} of the ice-free land. In 1900, {{forest.cleared_1900_pct|pc}} of it was under crops, pasture or converted rangeland; in 2023, {{forest.cleared_2023_pct|pc}}. Net, farming took another {{forest.net_km2|m1}} million km² of the forest zone.

    Share of the natural forest zone in farming use, by region

    Per cent of the forest zone under cropland, pasture or converted rangeland. Bar: 2023; tick: 1900. HYDE 3.3.

    That net figure hides two movements. Across the forest zone, {{forest.gross_gain_km2|m1}} million km² went into farming in places where farming grew, and {{forest.gross_loss_km2|m1}} million km² came out of it in places where farming shrank. Brazil cleared the most (+{{change.top_clear_gain.0.v|absint}} km² in farming use), then China, Indonesia, Australia and India. The land that came out of farming is in the old farm countries: {{change.gross_loss_share_us_europe_russia_pct|pc}} of it is in Europe, Russia and the United States. In the eastern United States, east of 90°W (roughly the Mississippi), farmed land fell from {{change.east_us.c1900_pct|pc}} of the forest zone in 1900 to {{change.east_us.c2023_pct|pc}} now, as farms were given up from New England to the Appalachians and much of the land grew back to woods. In Europe it fell from {{change.europe.cleared_1900_pct|pc}} to {{change.europe.cleared_2023_pct|pc}}, with the largest declines in France, Poland, Italy and Germany.

    The forests that came back are in the countries that cleared theirs first. The forests going now are in the tropics.

    How does that compare with what satellites see? MODIS, classifying the land at 0.05 degrees in 2019, finds closed forest on {{modis.forest_in_zone_pct|pc}} of HYDE's forest zone, and forest or woody savanna on {{modis.treecover_in_zone_pct|pc}}, below HYDE's {{forest.uncleared_2023_pct|pc}} unfarmed. The difference is land that is not farmed but not forest either: logged, burnt, degraded or naturally open. Cell by cell, the share HYDE has farmed and the share MODIS finds without trees agree only loosely (correlation {{modis.r_cleared_vs_nontree|f2}} across {{modis.n_cells|int}} quarter-degree cells). MODIS's cropland, {{modis.crop_km2|m1}} million km², is {{modis.crop_vs_hyde_pct|pc}} of HYDE's for 2023. Remember both when reading the forest layer: it shows farming in the forest zone, not forest.

    05 · Wild, seminatural, usedThe wild was left alone; the middle was taken

    The anthromes put every cell into one of 20 classes by its people and its land use: dense settlements, villages, croplands, rangelands, seminatural lands (with some people or use, but mostly natural) and wildlands (no people, no use). In 1900, {{anthromes.used_1900_pct|pc}} of the ice-free land was in the first four, used anthromes. By 2023 it was {{anthromes.used_2023_pct|pc}}.

    Surprisingly, the wildlands barely moved: {{anthromes.wild_1900_pct|pc}} of the ice-free land in 1900, {{anthromes.wild_2023_pct|pc}} now. Of the land that was wild in 1900, {{anthromes.wild1900_to.wild|pc}} is still classed wild. It is the land that is too cold, too dry or too remote to farm: the Sahara and the Arabian and Australian deserts, the tundra, and the boreal forests of Canada and Siberia, where {{anthromes.wildwood_2023_north50_pct|pc}} of the remaining wild woodlands lie north of 50°N. The great loss was in the middle, the seminatural lands, inhabited but mostly natural, which shrank from {{anthromes.semi_1900_pct|pc}} of the ice-free land to {{anthromes.semi_2023_pct|pc}} as they were turned into croplands, rangelands and villages. Dense settlements, cities and their surroundings, grew {{anthromes.settle_x|f1}} times over, to {{anthromes.settle_2023_pct|pc1}} of the land.

    06 · What the data can sayA model of the past, not a photograph

    Nobody mapped the world's fields in 1900. HYDE starts from statistics: national and regional censuses of agriculture where they exist, the FAO since 1961, and for earlier years estimates from population and land per person. It then spreads each country's total over its grid using rules: cropland goes where people live and the soil and climate suit crops; grazing goes where it is dry or empty. So the 1900 map is a model. Country totals are better than the pattern inside a country, and a single 9 km cell should not be read literally. The uncertainty grows with distance from good statistics: in 1900 that means much of Africa, Asia and South America.

    There are other limits. HYDE has no forest of its own: its forest zone is potential natural vegetation, and "cleared" means farmed, so logging, fire and plantations do not show. Grazing land includes vast natural rangelands. And HYDE 3.3 has a baseline, a lower and an upper estimate; I use the baseline. The present-day check uses MODIS 2019, a satellite classification with its own errors, at a different time and with different definitions.

    07 · How I did thisMethods and sources

    Farmland. I summed HYDE 3.3 baseline cropland, pasture, rangeland and converted rangeland (km² per 5 arc-minute cell) for 1900 and 2023. Grazing land is the last three together, as in HYDE. Shares are of HYDE's maximum land area; "ice-free" removes land in the anthrome class "ice, uninhabited". Forest zone. HYDE's general file of potential forest (from the WWF biome map) marks the forest zone; cropland, pasture and converted rangeland inside it count as farmed. Gross gains and losses are the sums of cell-by-cell increases and decreases. Countries and regions come from HYDE's country grid, named with Natural Earth. Anthromes are HYDE's own maps for 1900 and 2023. MODIS: the MCD12C1 IGBP classes for 2019, compared with HYDE on a quarter-degree grid. The map shows all of this on a 10 arc-minute grid (about 18 km). Every number on this page is computed by the scripts in the repository and checked against data/results.json.
